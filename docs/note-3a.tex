\begin{abstract}
The Jacobian conjecture fails in dimension $n \ge 3$: the July 2026
counterexample of Alp\"oge--Fable is a Keller map of $\C^3$ built from a
rational coordinate whose poles are hidden on a hyperplane and cancelled by a
ramified composition. We study whether this mechanism --- \emph{triangular
pole hiding} --- can operate in the plane, where the conjecture remains open.
We formalize the mechanism as a \emph{scaffold} over a pole curve
$\Gamma \subset \C^2$ and prove two sterility theorems. Over the hyperbola
$\Gamma = \{xy = 1\}$ no scaffold exists at all. Over the line
$\Gamma = \{x = 0\}$, every scaffold in every stratum of an explicit,
machine-verified partition of the configuration space save one named sliver is
an affine triangular automorphism; a scaffold outside the tame side would
\emph{be} a counterexample to the two-dimensional conjecture. In the remaining
sliver (composite-jewel cells) we prove the minimal charts pair-barren,
exhibit the stratum's genuine pairs (a shear orbit of the germ), prove every
scaffold on that orbit impossible at every $P$-degree, and confine any
survivor to non-shear tail deformations with $\deg_W P \ge 4$ and
$3 \mid J$. The proofs run on a small amount of new machinery --- a graded
fat-edge kill, a uniform residue theorem with ratio $-1/(N-1)$ and a coset
refinement, a trace identity, and fertility/residue laws for composite cone
edges --- each of which ships with an exact-arithmetic machine certificate.
Byte-frozen copies of the two core theorem documents were hash-committed
publicly on July 23, 2026, prior to this note.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mechanism}

\textbf{History.} The temptation dates to the first wrong proof: Kraus's 1884
argument \cite{Kraus1884,RD2025} failed exactly at controlling ramification at
infinity. Vitushkin \cite{Vitushkin1999} constructed the honest prototype: a
rational map of $\C^2$ with constant Jacobian determinant $-2$ that is
non-injective --- legal only because one coordinate carries a pole, on a line.
For twenty-seven years the question was whether the pole can be
\emph{cancelled} without destroying the constant Jacobian. In July 2026 it was
answered in dimension~3: the map
\begin{align*}
F(x,y,z) = \bigl(&(1+xy)^3 z + y^2(1+xy)(4+3xy),\\
&y + 3x(1+xy)^2 z + 3xy^2(4+3xy),\ \ 2x - 3x^2y - x^3z\bigr),
\end{align*}
announced by Alp\"oge and derived by the Claude model Fable \cite{Alpoge2026},
is a polynomial Keller map ($\det JF \equiv -2$) with an explicit fiber
collision; we have verified both facts in exact arithmetic (\S5).
Structurally $F = H \circ G$ where $G$ carries the rational coordinate
$w = y + 1/x$ (poles on the hyperplane $\{x = 0\}$) and $H$ is a $3\!:\!1$
ramified cubic whose Jacobians cancel to $-2$
\cite{SBS2026,Tao2026,Gallagher2026}. The conjecture is therefore false for
all $n \ge 3$ (and the Dixmier conjecture falls by stable equivalence
\cite{Zhang2026}); dimension~2 remains open.

\textbf{The plane question.} The published lenses on why $F$ works --- Tao's
multiplication-map digestion \cite{Tao2026}, the Sawin--Levinson
$\mathbb{A}^1$-bundle picture and Gallagher's non-properness account
\cite{Gallagher2026} --- all use the third dimension as room to hide
ramification. Our question is the mechanism's shadow in $\C^2$: can a
rational coordinate with poles on a curve $\Gamma$ be polynomially recombined
into a plane Keller map? The pole locus $\Gamma$ plays the role of the
Jelonek non-properness set.

\section{Scaffolds and the dichotomy}

\begin{definition}
Fix a curve $\Gamma \subset \C^2$. A \emph{scaffold over $\Gamma$} is a
triple $(w, c, P)$ with $w \in \C(x,y)$ having poles exactly on $\Gamma$;
$c \in \C[x,y]$; and $P \in \C[W, C]$ such that $f := P(w, c) \in \C[x,y]$
(the poles cancel) and $\det \J(f, c) = \kappa \in \C^{*}$. Write
$D := \deg_W P$.
\end{definition}

Over the line $\Gamma = \{x=0\}$, normalize $w = a/x^{d}$ with $x \nmid a$
and $c = x\,u$. The chain rule forces the \emph{pair equation}
\[
\J(w, c) = \lambda x^{m}, \qquad \lambda \in \C^{*},\ m \in \mathbb{Z},
\]
and differentiating $f = P(w,c)$ gives the \emph{master identity}
$P_W(w, c) = \mu x^{-m}$ with $\mu = \kappa/\lambda$. Pairs $(w,c)$
satisfying the pair equation are the raw material; scaffolds are pairs that
additionally support $P$. Killing pairs kills scaffolds; several strata
contain genuine pairs that die only at the $P$-layer.

\begin{theorem}[Line Dichotomy]
For any line scaffold, $u(0,y)$ is forced to be a nonzero constant
$\gamma$, and exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $u$ is constant, and the scaffold is an affine triangular
automorphism; or
\item $u$ is nonconstant, and $F = (f, c)$ is a Keller map of $\C^2$ that
is not injective: the fiber $\{c = 0\} = \{x = 0\} \sqcup \{u = 0\}$ has two
components glued by $P$. Such a scaffold \emph{is} a counterexample to the
two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

So the stakes are exact: proving every line scaffold lands in case (1) is
precisely proving this mechanism cannot falsify the plane conjecture over a
line.

\section{The two theorems}

\begin{theoremA}
Over $\Gamma = \{xy = 1\}$ no scaffold exists at all.
\end{theoremA}

\begin{theoremB}
Partition line configurations by the fiber hull of $c - t$ at $x \to 0$
(the lower-left Newton hull from the $-t$ vertex $(0,0)$ to the top vertex
$(\alpha, N)$, $N := \deg_y c$). The partition of Table~\ref{tab:strata} is
complete --- the classifier is executable, and a $2{,}100$-shape randomized
sweep lands every candidate in exactly one stratum (\S5). In every stratum
except E4, no scaffold with nonconstant $u$ exists; consequently every
scaffold there is an affine triangular automorphism.
\end{theoremB}

\textbf{The sliver, honestly scoped.} E4 is nonempty as a \emph{pair}
stratum: at the first fertile family ($(N, r) = (3, 6)$, interior cone
coefficient $t = \pm\sqrt3$, pole depths $k \equiv 1 \pmod 3$;
machine-confirmed $k \in \{1, 4, 7\}$) the local germ exists in closed form,
$w_{\mathrm{germ}} = -\lambda(x^{-1} + \sqrt3\, x^2 Y)$ at $k = 1$, and the
shear $S \colon Y \mapsto Y + \gamma x$ carries it to a genuine pair family
--- the composite analog of the binomial shear orbit. We prove:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the residue ratios at the cone's punctures are primitive cube roots of
unity, so the residue clash kills every scaffold with $3 \nmid J$;
\item the character/divisor bound confines survivors to $D \ge 4$;
\item minimal charts (no tails beyond the forced $\gamma x$) are barren at
the pair level: the bucket march terminates with an unshieldable cokernel
obstruction at the germ's first $\gamma$-rung, with core $(k-2)\,t\gamma\lambda$,
at $k = 1, 4, 7$ and both signs of $t$;
\item \emph{the entire shear locus is scaffold-dead, for every realization
$\sigma$ and every $P$-degree}: the master identity decouples over the germ
pair's weight grading; functional independence ($\J(w,c) \neq 0$) forces
every non-target weight line of $P_W$ to vanish as a polynomial; a nonzero
$\mu$ then forces the single coefficient $a_{\sigma-1,0} \neq 0$, whose
contribution to the sheared $f \circ S$ is an uncancellable leading pole
$a_{\sigma-1,0}(-\lambda)^{\sigma-1} x^{-(\sigma-1)}$ along $Y = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
What remains open of the entire program: non-shear tail deformations of
these pairs --- nonempty at the pair level (an exact window candidate
exists) and confined to $D \ge 4$ with $3 \mid J$ --- which we expect to
fall to the $\xi$-$\psi$-calculus, the cone ODE being first order in $\xi$.
Every other stratum is closed.

\begin{corollary*}
The mechanism that falsifies the Jacobian conjecture in dimension~3 cannot
produce a plane counterexample over the standard hyperbola, nor over a line
outside stratum E4 --- and inside E4 only via non-shear tail deformations
with $D \ge 4$ and $3 \mid J$.
\end{corollary*}

\textbf{Not claimed:} other pole curves (general $\C^{*}$-embeddings, higher
genus, reducible $\Gamma$), non-triangular compositions, or the
two-dimensional conjecture itself.

\section{The new machinery (statements)}

\begin{theorem}[Fat-Edge Kill / Staircase Sterility]
Suppose the support of $c$ satisfies the strict collapse hypothesis for the
grading $\omega(i, j) = Ni - \alpha j$: $\omega > 0$ off the top vertex
$b\,x^{\alpha}y^{N}$. Then for every line candidate $w$,
$\J(w, c) \notin \C[x, 1/x]$ --- no pair, for any $m \in \mathbb{Z}$, zero
included. The proof is one page: the top-vertex bracket
$L_0 = \J(\,\cdot\,, b x^{\alpha} y^{N})$ is diagonal in the monomial basis
with eigenvalue $b\,\omega$ and shifts $y$-degree by $N-1$. A verbatim
$\omega_1$-graded variant (Theorem$'$) kills bare-ramified first edges.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Residue]
In every fertile binomial cell $(N, k, r)$ --- fertility being exactly
$r \mid Nk$ and $r \nmid k$ --- the normalized germ residue ratio at every
puncture is $-1/(N-1)$, independent of $k$, $r$, and all tails. The induced
clash forces $(N-1)^J = 1$ against the master identity, killing all
scaffolds in stratum E1 with $N \ge 3$; $N = 2$ (ratio $-1$, silent on odd
$D$) falls to the jewel ladder and parity-pole argument instead. At
$d = \gcd(N-1, r) > 1$ the germ decomposes over $\zeta_d$-cosets; the
operator preserves cosets and a kernel-exclusion argument closes the
refinement (witnessed at $(3,2,6)$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Composite-cone laws]
With interior cone-edge support (cone polynomial $E(\xi)$,
$d = \gcd(N-1, r) > 1$): the germ recursion acquires a termination condition
$P_k(t) = 0$, an explicit polynomial per $k$ (at $(3,6)$:
$P_1 \propto t^2 - 3$, $P_2 \propto t(2t^2-9)$), off whose zero locus the
cone is germ-barren (closing E3); on it, the per-root residue ratio is
\[
\frac{\varphi(\rho)}{\varphi(0)} = \frac{d}{(N-1)\,\rho\,E'(\rho)},
\]
whose values at the first fertile family are the primitive cube roots of
unity above. The binomial constant $-1/(N-1)$ is the special case
$E = 1 + \xi^d$ evaluated at its roots.
\end{theorem}

Alongside these run a trace identity (sum of residues over each cone
fiber's punctures vanishes), the divisor-law/no-conspiracy telescoping for
multi-edge staircases, and the sharpness objects: the $N = 2$ \emph{jewel}
pairs (e.g.\ $w^2 - 4c = x^{-2}$ with involution
$(x,y) \mapsto (-x,\, y + 1/x^5)$), the shear orbits, and the
composite-jewel germs. All die at the $P$-layer or in the confined sliver
--- none assembles into a counterexample.

\section{Relation to prior and concurrent work}

Gwo\'zdziewicz \cite{Gwozdziewicz1993} proved a Keller map injective on one
line is an automorphism --- a foil: our theorems constrain the
\emph{mechanism}, not the map's restriction. The Newton-polygon school
(Garc\'ia Vicente--Gonz\'alez Villa et al., Makar-Limanov) supplies the
polygon vocabulary; our fiber hull, gradings, and residue calculus run at
the pair-equation level the polygon alone does not see, and the school's own
papers state that boundary. Orevkov's analytic realizations of Vitushkin's
covering \cite{Orevkov} are the closest classical geometry to the
pole-hiding lens. Concurrently with this note, Shaska \cite{Shaska2026}
proved every $\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant (graded) Keller map of $\C^2$ is an
automorphism; the class boundary is exactly our subject --- scaffolds are
not graded, and the tails that break equivariance are where jewels, shears,
and the sliver live. On the fiber-topology side we use
Abhyankar--Moh--Suzuki, Zaidenberg--Lin, and Kaliman's classification in the
$D = 1$ closure, and Masuda--Miyanishi sits adjacent to the jewel geometry.
To our knowledge the sterility theorems, the fat-edge kill, the uniform
residue law with its coset refinement, the trace identity, and the
composite-cone fertility/residue laws are new.
